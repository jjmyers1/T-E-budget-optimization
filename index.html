THE CHALLENGE

A single cost index could not tell the full story.

The existing planning approach relied heavily on a basic cost signal. That made it difficult to account for markets with similar staffing but very different service geographies, customer concentrations, local prices, and role-level travel requirements.

My objective was to create an easy-to-use, repeatable process that separated cost pressure from travel difficulty and could be integrated into existing planning templates.

MODELING APPROACH

From raw operating differences to a clearer planning signal.

I combined four business drivers and tested the resulting indexes for outliers, sensitivity, and usability.

01

Customer density

Captured the concentration of demand and service activity.

02

Cost of living

Reflected local price pressure affecting trip costs.

03

Travel distance

Measured separation across each service geography.

04

Workforce mix

Accounted for role-level needs and headcount composition.

  1. AuditCompared the legacy signal with market characteristics.
  2. BuildCreated separate cost and travel-difficulty indexes.
  3. Stress-testEvaluated distributions, outliers, and ranking sensitivity.
  4. CompareFlagged potential over- and under-allocation.
  5. OperationalizeStructured outputs for recurring planning cycles.

ANALYTICAL JUDGMENT

Non-contiguous geographies created artificial distance spikes. I evaluated log transformation, cube-root transformation, and capping to reduce outlier influence without flattening meaningful business differences.

Preserve the business signal, not just the distribution.

RESULTS & IMPACT
