THE CHALLENGE

A single cost index could not tell the full story.

The existing planning approach relied heavily on a basic cost index. That made it difficult to distinguish regions that required more resources from those that could operate efficiently with less, especially when staffing, geography, customer concentration, local prices, and role-level travel needs differed.

My objective was to create an easy-to-use, repeatable process that separated cost from travel difficulty and improved how budgets and headcount were allocated. The approach directed resources toward the regions that needed them while identifying areas where spending could be reduced or rebalanced.

MODELING APPROACH

Turning operating differences into clearer planning indexes.

I combined four business drivers and tested the resulting indexes for outliers, sensitivity, and usability.

01

Customer density

Captured the concentration of demand and service activity.

02

Cost of living

Reflected local price pressure affecting trip costs.

03

Travel distance

Measured separation across each service geography.

04

Workforce mix

Accounted for role-level needs and headcount composition.

  1. AuditCompared the existing index with market characteristics.
  2. BuildCreated separate cost and travel-difficulty indexes.
  3. Stress-testEvaluated distributions, outliers, and ranking sensitivity.
  4. CompareFlagged potential over- and under-allocation.
  5. OperationalizeStructured outputs for recurring planning cycles.

ANALYTICAL JUDGMENT

Non-contiguous geographies created artificial distance spikes. I evaluated log transformation, cube-root transformation, and capping to reduce outlier influence without flattening meaningful business differences.

Preserve the business value of the index, not just the distribution.

RESULTS & IMPACT

Better allocation. Clearer decisions. A repeatable process.

01

Headcount allocation needed greater efficiency

Some regions required additional support, while others could operate with less, showing the value of aligning headcount with regional demand.

02

Cost and distance required separate indexes

Using separate indexes clarified whether local prices or service geography drove each region's needs.

03

The existing index could over- or under-allocate funds

The multi-factor approach identified both higher and lower resource needs that one cost measure could miss.

DELIVERABLE

A practical allocation framework

  • Two complementary planning indexes
  • A balanced view of higher and lower regional needs
  • A process to refresh inputs each planning cycle
  • Outputs designed for adoption in existing templates
